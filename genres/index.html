Music Genres

Music is split into hundreds of genres and sub-genres, and usually differentiated by melody, lyricism, or how it was made.

To better learn about genres use our article!

Music requires context, whether it's just for fun, as a protest against an agenda, or a personal way to vent the artist's feelings in the form of a song. A song doesn't need to have lyrics, but instrumentals tend to be less popular than songs with actual lyrics. Returning to the idea of music as a way to vent the artist's feelings, this can most commonly be found in love or heartbreak songs from artists like Taylor Swift, Malcolm Todd, and Matt Maltese. These artists gain a following or liking by many fans because of their catchy tunes or relatable lyrics. The former is a personal reason for me (the writer of this article) and my connection to Matt Maltese. In simple terms, if you would like to create music, it's good to identify the theme or context of your music in order to identify what genre of music you would like to make. There's no shame in creating a song without much meaning or just for fun as a hobby; just enjoy what you create and don't treat it like a chore. Hard work may be overshadowed by talent, but talent is not nearly as impressive as hard work.

Pop

Pop music is built to connect with a broad audience. It often features memorable melodies, clear song structures, catchy hooks, and polished production. Pop draws ideas from many other genres, so its sound changes with the times.

COMMON FEATURES

Catchy hooks · memorable melodies · verse-and-chorus structure
